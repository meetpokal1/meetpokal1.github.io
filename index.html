MarketPulse India: Wholesale Commodity Price Intelligence & Mandi Econometrics

CI verified · 26 tests · Published on Tableau Public

Market Problem: Agricultural wholesale prices across India exhibit extreme spatial dispersion, severe reporting friction, and apparent elasticity paradoxes. Without accounting for variety confounding, transport friction, and frozen-price reporting habits across 1.48M records, market participants and policymakers make decisions on distorted market signals. MarketPulse India evaluates these discrepancies through reproducible DuckDB pipelines, panel econometrics, an interactive Streamlit scenario lab, and a published Tableau Public intelligence suite.

WHO IT IS FOR

Commodity traders, wholesale procurement teams, agricultural analysts, and supply-chain researchers evaluating mandi pricing across states.

HOW TEAMS USE IT

Screen candidate sourcing corridors, evaluate spatial price spreads against modeled freight buffers (₹120/Qtl baseline), and filter out frozen-price reporting artifacts before committing capital.

DECISION BENEFIT & BOUNDARIES

Prioritizes high-conviction procurement investigations and prevents decisions based on distorted quotes. Caveat: Spreads are not automatic arbitrage; freight is modeled; and panel findings are observational.

SPATIAL ARBITRAGE FINDING

Terminal markets trade at persistent discounts to farm-gate hubs

The Lasalgaon-to-Mumbai onion corridor exhibits a mean spread of -₹157.76/Qtl across 507 overlapping trading days, inverted on 74.75% of days (379 days). Observed price spreads alone do not imply profitable physical arbitrage: Mumbai wholesale quotes reflect cultivar heterogeneity (generic mixed grades vs. Lasalgaon export-grade Unhali/Red), arrival timing lags, and handling frictions rather than direct farm-gate parity.

ECONOMETRIC SPECIFICATION

Two-Way Fixed Effects estimates an inelastic spot price response

A pooled OLS regression estimates an arrival–price coefficient of -0.1008 across 197,941 observations. Introducing Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE) to control for unobserved mandi-specific characteristics and calendar-month time shocks across 394 market clusters yields an estimated reduced-form arrival–price elasticity of -0.0360 (cluster-robust SE = 0.0183, t = -1.96, p < 0.05, 95% CI [-0.0720, -0.0001]). This indicates an inelastic short-run spot price response (< 0.4% price reaction to a 10% arrival shift), refuting assumptions of massive spot price collapse.

RELIABILITY FORENSICS

Unchanged price streaks reflect administrative boards and price stickiness

Forensic audit of 1,481,324 observations identified 27,420 streak instances where mandis reported identical modal prices across seven or more consecutive trading dates (median: 11 days, max: 518 days). Rather than evidence that trading ceased, the diagnostic pipeline classifies 69.2% of streaks under the explanatory hypothesis of administrative price ceilings (daily board quotes) and 29.5% under candidate price stickiness. DuckDB quality gates quarantined 74 defective records (70 low-price scale errors below ₹10/Qtl and 4 zero-arrival entries), preserving 1,481,250 valid observations (99.9950% conservation rate) for analytical modeling.

Dataset provenance & hygiene: The dataset comprises 1,481,324 daily wholesale observations across six western and southern states—Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala (30 Aug 2024 – 28 Sep 2026). DuckDB zero-copy data quality gates audited 27,420 unchanged price streaks (≥ 7 days, max 518 days) and quarantined 74 exceptions (70 unit-scale low-price reporting errors below ₹10/Qtl and 4 zero-arrival records), establishing an audit-clean 99.9950% valid analytical foundation (1,481,250 rows).

Reproducible data contract: The Tableau deliverable is powered by six typed, deterministically sorted analytical extracts governed by an explicit manifest and verified by 26 passing automated pytest tests. The regression suite validates record conservation, monotonic date coverage, price non-negativity, and corridor relationship integrity.

Econometric methodology: Pooled OLS estimates an arrival–price coefficient of -0.1008 on 197,941 Onion observations. Applying a Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE) model with market entity fixed effects and calendar-month time fixed effects across 394 market clusters yields an estimated reduced-form arrival–price elasticity of -0.0360 (cluster-robust SE = 0.0183, t = -1.96, p < 0.05, 95% CI [-0.0720, -0.0001]). This observational reduced-form estimate reflects short-run within-market spot price clearing rather than structural demand elasticity, refuting hypotheses of extreme short-run price collapse.
